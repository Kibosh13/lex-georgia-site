The region

Why the country is a question of its own

In much of Latin America refugee status is not limited to the 1951 Convention. The Cartagena Declaration widens the approach: besides persecution for political, religious and similar reasons, many states also look at generalised violence, internal conflict and serious human-rights violations. How that is written into national law is checked country by country.

Filing deadlines, the authority and whether a claim can be sent remotely all differ. Choosing the wrong country of entry is costly, so the jurisdiction is settled before travel, not after.

What a procedure usually includes

  • A claim to a refugee commission or the migration authority
  • No return to the country of risk while the case is pending
  • The file is not shared with the country of origin
  • A decision, and a way to appeal it
