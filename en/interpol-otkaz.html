If the data stay

A refusal from the Commission does not end the matter by itself. We look at why the data were kept and which further steps still make sense.

After a refusal

Why the Commission keeps the data

Complaints are most often rejected not because the notice is lawful but because the arguments were not proven: documents were missing, the position was general, or procedure was breached. Such mistakes can be corrected.

We read the Commission’s decision, compare it with the complaint file and identify which route can realistically change the situation: the CCF, the courts or international protection mechanisms.

Common reasons for refusal

  • Arguments not backed by documents
  • Political character not proven
  • Missing translations or filing errors
  • New circumstances not considered
  • Missed deadlines and Commission queries
Options

What can still be done

Request for revision

If facts emerge that could have changed the decision, the Commission can be asked to revise it within 6 months of their discovery.

New complaint

If the situation has changed, for example refugee status, an expired limitation period or a dropped case, we prepare a new filing.

Courts at home

Lifting the arrest order or ending the case in the requesting country is a ground for deletion.

Extradition defence

If the person is arrested, we defend them in the requested state’s court: political motive, torture risk, unfair trial.

ECHR and UN bodies

Where extradition is imminent, interim measures can be sought from the ECHR, the UN Committee against Torture or the Human Rights Committee.

Asylum

International protection in a third country lowers the extradition risk and strengthens the case before the Commission.

Questions

Common questions after a refusal

Can a Commission decision be appealed?

CCF decisions are final, but a request for revision is allowed if new material facts emerge.

When can I file again?

There is no fixed waiting period for a new complaint; it must rest on new circumstances rather than repeat earlier arguments.

What if I am arrested after a refusal?

Contact a lawyer at once: extradition proceedings run on short deadlines, and the court of the host country decides on surrender.

We assess the prospects honestly. If a further filing makes no sense, we say so and propose other means of protection.
